# Problem Statement: Multilingual Policy, Rights & Scheme Explainer
**IDEA//FORGE Hackathon | Track 1, Problem 1**

## Core Problem
Millions of people in India, especially rural, low-literacy and non-English speakers (farmers, women, students, senior citizens), miss welfare benefits because government notices and scheme rules are written in complex English/legalese, spread across many websites, and hard to check for eligibility. SchemeSaathi explains schemes, notices and rights in simple language in English, Hindi, Kannada and Tamil, by text and by voice, checks eligibility, and tells people exactly which documents to carry and where to apply.

---

## Who Faces This Problem?
1. **Marginalized & Smallholder Farmers:** Struggling with bureaucratic eligibility definitions under direct benefit transfer programs like PM-KISAN, often unaware whether land size, khata title status, or minor tax filings exclude them.
2. **Rural Women & Homemakers:** Unaware of direct entitlement rights under initiatives like Gruha Lakshmi, PM Ujjwala Yojana, or Shakti free bus travel due to lack of local-language communication or unclear document prerequisites.
3. **Low-Literacy & Vernacular-First Citizens:** Citizens speaking Kannada, Tamil, or Hindi who cannot parse legalistic gazette notifications, government circulars, or English-only portal menus.
4. **First-Generation Students & Senior Citizens:** SC/ST students seeking Post-Matric scholarships or elders seeking universal healthcare (such as Ayushman Bharat Vay Vandana 70+ expansion) who struggle to identify required certifying authorities.

---

## Why It Matters: Ground Realities & Systemic Friction
- **The Information Asymmetry Gap:** While India has created world-leading Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT) and digital public infrastructure, information dissemination remains fragmented across dozens of departmental silos (Central ministries, state Seva Sindhu portals, district bulletins). *(Verify before use: Over hundreds of welfare schemes exist across central and state tiers).*
- **The Legalese & Literacy Hurdle:** Official administrative orders and gazette circulars are authored in formal, high-register vocabulary with cross-referenced statutory clauses. For an everyday citizen with a 5th or 6th-grade education, understanding an exclusion clause or appeal deadline is daunting.
- **The Middleman / Dalal Exploitation:** Because citizens cannot independently verify whether they are eligible or what exact documents are mandatory, they frequently fall prey to unauthorized middlemen charging exorbitant fees for basic welfare enrollments.
- **Privacy & Document Anxiety:** Rural beneficiaries are often apprehensive about sharing sensitive national IDs (such as Aadhaar) with unverified digital apps. A zero-retention, client-side evaluation architecture is critical for public trust.
